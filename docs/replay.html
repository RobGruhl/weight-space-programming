03 / Behavioral replay

One rescue
in four questions.

Baseline finished three questions correctly. The exact published patch finished all four. The shuffled-direction control also finished three, but failed to finish a different question.

A development replay, not a benchmark.

These four synthetic questions were used before this run. There was one generation seed and an 8,192-token cap. This is not independent edit discovery or a fresh holdout evaluation.

What changed on the rescued question?

The baseline rectangle answer repeated a 12-token block 253 times and exhausted its budget. The exact patch completed correctly in 1,678 tokens. The shuffled control also completed that question, but exhausted its budget on addition.

The patch was not uniformly shorter: it generated more tokens than baseline on algebra and 2 + 2. These timings and token counts cannot establish a general speed improvement.

The detector missed the loops

The frozen detector checked exact 16-token blocks and flagged none of the twelve outputs. A later exploratory analysis searched periods 1–64 and found the repeated runs. That analysis is useful, but it did not replace the original endpoint or change the grading.

A stopping-token caveat

The local runtime stopped at <|endoftext|>, not <|im_end|>. Completed samples emitted exactly two extra tokens after <|im_end|>. Removing them would not change these completion categories, but upstream termination parity was not verified.
